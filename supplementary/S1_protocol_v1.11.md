# Review protocol — Fault-tolerant control of robot manipulators: linking fault models to accommodation strategies (FAME taxonomy)
Version 1.11 · 25 November 2026 · Paper R1 (target journal: Sensors, MDPI, article type Review)
Status: fixed before the reported searches (RS2). Later changes are dated amendments (Section 13).
Language: English, so that this file can be published unchanged as Supplementary File S1.
Working title (14 words): "Fault-Tolerant Control of Robot Manipulators: A Systematic Review Linking Fault Models to Accommodation Strategies". The asset name "FAME taxonomy" is used identically in abstract, keywords, figures, repository and README.

## 1. Review type and reporting standards
- Systematic review with a mapping layer and a synthesis layer, reported with PRISMA 2020, PRISMA 2020 for Abstracts and PRISMA-S. Completed checklists: Supplementary Files S7 and S8.
- Registration: PROSPERO does not accept engineering topics. Version 1.0 was fixed on 15-11-2026, before the searches run that same day. The protocol was registered on OSF Registries on 16-11-2026 (https://osf.io/ecqa7, DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/ECQA7; retrospective registration): after the RS2 searches and exports (15-11-2026) and after most of the L3 full-text extraction, but before the blind human coding (Section 7, S10) and before the synthesis (RS4) and the tests of H1-H3. - The review is a synthesis. It reports no new controller, experiment or primary dataset.
- Single-author review: screening and coding are rule-based and scripted, validated by blind human coding (Section 7). This is declared as a limitation.

## 2. Rationale
- Reviews of fault-tolerant control (FTC) of manipulators are organized by method family (Milecki and Nowak 2023; Khan, Nasir and Mekid 2025; Quamar and Nasir 2026) or restricted to one fault and domain (Chen et al. 2023, locked joints in space manipulators). The foundational survey is Visinsky, Cavallaro and Walker (1994). Quamar and Nasir (2026) report a PRISMA flow diagram over three databases (320 papers) and a qualitative fault taxonomy (occurrence, severity, affected component, origin, signal impact), but give no search strings, no fault equations and no joint-level fault models (locked or free-swinging joints, loss of effectiveness), and do not relate fault models to accommodation strategies or grade the evidence. No review organizes the field by the mathematical model of the fault, and none publishes reproducible strings, scripted screening rules and a coded corpus.
- A controller is designed for a fault model, not for a method label. A reader who must accommodate a given fault (for example a locked elbow or a 40 % torque loss) cannot find, in the existing reviews, which strategies have been shown to accommodate that model, on which manipulator class and with which evidence.
- Related work by the author: Urrea (2025, Actuators 14, 177) reviewed hybrid FTC at the level of cooperative multi-robot systems and reported that physical validation covers about one quarter of the strategies (an estimate cited from earlier work; wording corrected in amendment 1.10). The present review does not revisit that system level: it works at the level of the fault model of a single manipulator and grades the evidence study by study. Multi-robot team FTC is out of scope and is referred to that review.
- Reporting checklists exist for fault detection and diagnosis (Maican et al. 2025, Actuators 14, 566). None exists for fault injection in fault-tolerant control experiments.
- Scoping counts (WoS, 15-11-2026): 345 core FTC records on manipulators in 2023-2026; 113 (33 %) mention experiments or hardware; 9 (3 %) mention benchmarks, fault injection, datasets or reproducibility.

## 3. Objective and research questions
Objective: to establish which accommodation strategies have been shown to handle each actuator and joint fault model in robot manipulators, under which structural conditions and with which evidence, and to deliver the FAME taxonomy, a normalized fault-model library and a fault-injection reporting checklist.
Primary questions:
- RQ1 (F). Which actuator and joint fault models are used, and how are they written in one normalized form?
- RQ2 (F x A). Which accommodation strategies have been applied to each fault model, and which structural conditions (remaining actuation authority, kinematic or actuation redundancy) and which sensing and fault information (measured signals, detection or estimation, admissible delay) does each pairing require?
Secondary questions:
- RQ3 (M, time). How does the F x A map differ across manipulator classes and periods?
- RQ4 (E, reporting). With which level of evidence is each F x A pairing supported, and how are fault scenarios reported?
- RQ5 (Agenda). Which F x A cells are empty or supported only by simulation, and what research agenda follows?
Pre-specified hypotheses (confirmatory; all other associations are exploratory):
- H1. Joint-level faults (F4, F5) are validated on physical hardware more often than multiplicative actuator faults (F1).
- H2. Learning-based accommodation (A4) is validated on physical hardware less often than the other families.
- H3. The share of physically validated studies did not increase from 2016-2020 to 2021-2026.

## 4. The FAME coding frame
Codes, definitions and detection rules: CODEBOOK_FAME.csv (same version).
### 4.1 F, fault model, and the normalized form
Manipulator model: M(q) qdd + C(q,qd) qd + g(q) + f(qd) = tau_a + d, with commanded torque tau_c and applied torque tau_a.
Actuator-level map, channel i: tau_a,i = (1 - s_i) sat_[u_i(t)] ( lambda_i(t) tau_c,i + b_i(t) ) + s_i tau_bar_i.
Joint-level constraint: qd_i = 0 and q_i = q_i(t_f) for t >= t_f.
- F1 loss of effectiveness: 0 < lambda_i < 1.
- F2 additive bias or drift: b_i != 0.
- F3 reduced torque bound: u_i(t) below the nominal limit (saturation caused or aggravated by the fault; nominal input saturation alone is not a fault).
- F4 stuck actuator or locked joint. Sub-codes assigned at full text: F4t stuck-at torque (s_i = 1, tau_bar_i fixed) and F4q locked joint (joint-level constraint; one degree of freedom is lost).
- F5 free-swinging joint or total loss of torque: lambda_i = 0 and b_i = 0 (the arm becomes underactuated).
- F6 intermittent or time-varying fault: lambda_i, b_i or s_i switch or vary after onset.
- F7 transmission fault: the motor-to-joint map changes (gear or reducer damage, backlash growth, belt or cable failure). Sub-codes assigned at full text (amendment 1.10): F7c, broken or damaged cable of a cable-driven robot (an actuation channel is lost); F7r, reducer, backlash or wear fault of the transmission.
- F8 sensor fault (secondary class): y_i = q_i + delta_i, or the analogous form for velocity, torque or force sensing.
- F0 actuator or joint fault named without a model.
Time profile tag: abrupt, incipient, intermittent, unspecified. Multi-label. RS4 fixes the final notation and maps every extracted model onto this form or records why it does not fit.
### 4.2 A, accommodation family
A1 passive (fixed robust or adaptive law, no explicit fault information); A2 active (fault detection or estimation feeding compensation or reconfiguration); A3 redundancy-based (kinematic or actuation redundancy: redundancy resolution, torque reallocation, fault-tolerant configurations, motion replanning); A4 learning-based (the accommodation itself is learned). A1 and A2 are mutually exclusive for one scheme: when fault detection or estimation feeds the accommodation, the scheme is A2 even if its law is adaptive or robust (v1.8). Multi-label in the mapping set; one primary family per study in the synthesis set, assigned at full text by the mechanism that restores performance. Amendment 1.11(a), literal reading of the rule (applied on 25-11-2026 in stage E8-c2b, after the synthesis): a scheme is A2 when it computes online an estimate of the fault itself and that estimate enters the control law (the fault signal, a lumped term that the study states contains the fault, the effectiveness or its inverse, a bias or a stuck value), or when it detects or isolates the fault; it is A1 when it adapts only gains, bounds (including a bound on the fault magnitude or on the lumped term) or the weights of approximators of the fault-free dynamics, or adapts nothing. The reading was decided after the synthesis; the 65 studies first coded A1 were reread at full text and 27 were recoded A2, with the fault information made consistent with the family (Section 13.1). The mapping-set rules and codes are unchanged. Amendment 1.11(b), fault information apart from the family (applied on 25-11-2026 in stage E8-c3, after the synthesis): the family and the fault information are separate dimensions. A3 and A4 are assigned first, by the primary mechanism; between A1 and A2 the reading above applies, so "no explicit fault information" in the A1 definition means that the scheme neither estimates the fault online nor detects or isolates it. The fault information used by the scheme is coded apart, in the extraction sheet, as none, known (given to the controller a priori) or obtained (detection, isolation or estimation). A1 with known fault information is admitted and defined as a passive law with the fault given a priori (2 studies, Section 13.1).
### 4.3 M, manipulator class
M1 serial (including SCARA and industrial arms); M2 parallel (delta, Stewart, cable-driven parallel); M3 kinematically redundant serial; M4 space or free-floating; M5 collaborative, mobile, dual-arm or multi-arm manipulators treated as one platform with joint- or actuator-level faults; M6 other (continuum, soft).
### 4.4 E, evidence level (highest level reached)
E0 analysis only; E1 numerical simulation of a nominal model; E2 high-fidelity simulation (actuator dynamics, limits and noise, physics engine or co-simulation); E3 hardware bench or hardware-in-the-loop (real actuator or joint, not a full manipulator); E4 physical manipulator with injected or real faults.
In the mapping set, where only title, abstract and keywords are read, E is coded in three bins: simulation or analysis (E0-E2), bench (E3), physical manipulator (E4). The five levels are assigned only at full text.
### 4.5 Tags
T-ML (learning anywhere in the pipeline), T-DT (digital twin), T-OPEN (code or data stated as available), T-BENCH (named benchmark or fault-injection protocol), OWN (the author of this review is an author of the record).

## 5. Eligibility criteria
Inclusion (all must hold):
- IC1 Population: a robot manipulator of class M1-M6. A single joint or actuator rig counts only when presented as a manipulator joint.
- IC2 Fault: actuator fault (motor, drive, transmission), joint fault (locked, jammed, free-swinging) or, as secondary class, joint or force sensor fault.
- IC3 Intervention: an accommodation mechanism (control law, reconfiguration, compensation, redundancy resolution, motion replanning or learned policy) that preserves or recovers task performance under the fault.
- IC4 Record: peer-reviewed journal article, review or conference paper, in English, with an abstract.
Exclusion (reason code and rule ID recorded per record):
- EX-TYPE editorials, errata, notes, theses, book chapters, preprints, abstracts without full paper.
- EX-LANG non-English records (counted with strings F01e and SF01e; in-scope titles are listed in the supplement).
- EX-PLAT other platforms: aerial, ground, marine or space vehicles without a manipulator; legged robots and humanoids unless the fault is in an arm; exoskeletons and prostheses; motor drives or power converters outside a manipulator; beam or generic plant models that cite manipulators only as motivation.
- EX-SCOPE system-level FTC of multi-robot teams (formation, consensus, swarm, task reallocation among robots), where the fault is the loss or degradation of a robot and not a modeled actuator or joint fault. Dual-arm and cooperative manipulators with joint- or actuator-level fault models remain in scope (M5).
- EX-FAULT faults outside scope: communication and network faults, cyber-attacks, software faults, collisions treated as faults, payload variation without a fault.
- EX-FDI detection, diagnosis, isolation or prognosis without accommodation.
- EX-HW mechanism or hardware design with no control, switching or motion law.
- EX-DUP duplicates. Retracted records are marked RETRACTED and excluded from synthesis; records without an abstract are marked NO-ABS and handled in a sensitivity analysis.
Layer R (reviews): reviews that meet IC1-IC3 feed the comparison table and the snowball, not the counts of primary studies. Reviews identified by other methods (expert knowledge and the author's portfolio) enter layer R even when the strings do not retrieve them and are reported in the PRISMA box "records identified from other methods": Urrea (2025) and Maican et al. (2025).
Full-text eligibility rules (amendment 1.10; written after the synthesis, applied during the full-text extraction): (a) studies of generic nonlinear, stochastic, fractional-order, switched or Markov-jump systems are eligible only when a manipulator is the validation plant; they are flagged GEN (Euler-Lagrange formulations, which coincide with the manipulator model, are not flagged); (b) networks of manipulators are eligible when the fault acts on an actuator of an individual arm and is accommodated at that arm; the coordination of the team is not analyzed; flagged NET; (c) IC1 includes robotic mechanisms that position an end effector with actuated joints or rollers; IC3 includes safe degradation that brings the end effector to a safe pose; (d) a fault-tolerant configuration chosen at planning or kinematic-design level counts as A3 only if it defines the behavior of the arm after the fault; otherwise EX-HW.
Records by the author of this review: screened and coded by the same scripted rules, tagged OWN, never promoted by judgment into the synthesis set, and removed in a sensitivity analysis. The reference list cites at most five works of the author (at most 8 % of the list), each with a stated function.

## 6. Information sources and search
- Databases: Web of Science Core Collection (editions subscribed by the institution, recorded on execution) and Scopus. Google Scholar is not used because its results are not reproducible.
- Fields: WoS TS (title, abstract, author keywords, Keywords Plus); Scopus TITLE-ABS-KEY. The asymmetry is reported.
- Period: no lower limit; upper limit is the execution date (both databases on the same day). Mapping uses all years; synthesis uses 2016-2026.
- Exact strings: CADENAS_DEFINITIVAS_WOS.txt and CADENAS_DEFINITIVAS_SCOPUS.txt. Main corpus: F01 OR SF01. No NOT operator in the main strings; platform noise is removed by screening rules.
- Pilot (declared): on 15-11-2026 the main strings were run once to test syntax and controls, without export. WoS F01: 1509 records (1071 in 2016-2026; 765 in 2021-2026); the four known Actuators studies and the seed review of Khan et al. (2025) were retrieved; 120 records (8 %) carry multi-robot terms; 26 are reviews or carry review terms in the title. Scopus SF01: 2056 records (ratio to WoS 1.36). The reported search is the RS2 execution.
- Known-item tests before export: (i) every INCLUDE record of KNOWN_ITEMS_ACTUATORS.csv; (ii) the in-scope seed reviews of KNOWN_ITEMS_SEEDS.csv. A miss triggers a string amendment.
- Count controls: listed in the string files; a count outside its range is explained before exporting.
- Snowballing: backward (reference lists of the seed reviews) and forward (records citing the seed reviews, 2016-2026), with OpenAlex, which returns references, citing works and abstracts through one open interface, so that snowball records are screened with the same scripted rules as the corpus (amendment v1.5; OpenCitations, Crossref and Semantic Scholar remain as fallbacks).
- Supplementary sets (not part of the main corpus, screened with the same rules, reported in S2): (i) the non-English complement of SF01, to list in-scope non-English records; (ii) a post hoc Scopus query that replaces the manipulator block with the terms found missing by the snowball ("multi joint robot*", "robotic system*", "robot system*", "multi link robot*"), to bound the loss attributable to the string.

## 7. Selection process and validation
- Deduplication (scripted): exact DOI after normalization; otherwise normalized title with first-author surname and year within one year. Two records with different DOIs stay separate (conference and journal versions), except within the same document class and year. When the author field differs by format or typing error, an identical title of at least 40 characters with the same document class and year within one year is accepted (v1.5). WoS metadata is kept when both sources hold the record.
- Screening (scripted, on title, abstract and author keywords; database-assigned terms, Keywords Plus and Scopus Index Keywords, are not used because the author did not write them and they differ between sources, v1.5): INCLUDE, EXCLUDE (reason code, rule ID) or UNCERTAIN. Fixed rule order: EX-TYPE, EX-LANG, EX-PLAT, EX-SCOPE, EX-FAULT, EX-FDI, EX-HW, then IC1-IC3. UNCERTAIN records pass to a narrower second tier, defined after the blind human coding so that no rule is tuned on the validation sample; the residue stays in the mapping set with a flag and is removed in a sensitivity analysis. [Superseded by amendment 1.9(a): there is no second tier; the UNCERTAIN residue stays outside the primary mapping set and is added in a sensitivity analysis.]
- Operational form of IC1-IC3 (v1.5, fixed on the development set before human validation): IC1, a robot or manipulator term; IC3, a fault-tolerance or accommodation term; IC2, a specific fault term (F1-F8, or an actuator, joint, motor or sensor fault). INCLUDE when IC1 and IC3 hold and either IC2 holds or the IC3 term is in the title or the author keywords; also when IC1 holds, an actuator or joint fault is named and the title carries a control or planning term. IC1 and IC3 only in passing, or IC1 and IC2 without IC3, give UNCERTAIN. No IC1 term gives EXCLUDE.
- Layer R is assigned by rule: document type Review, or review, survey, overview or state of the art in the title.
- Rule development set: KNOWN_ITEMS_ACTUATORS.csv (17 records with expected decisions), the first 200 records by publication date and the records whose titles were inspected during the rule audit of 15-11-2026 (listed in DEV_inspeccionados_auditoria.csv). These records are excluded from the validation sample.
- Human validation: before any rule adjustment, the author codes blind a random sample of 60 records (seed recorded; stratified by rule outcome), without tools and without the key. Reported per dimension (inclusion, each F, A and M code as a binary label, E bin): Cohen's kappa with bootstrap 95 % interval, Gwet's AC1 (robust to low prevalence) and raw agreement. Kappa below 0.60 is reported as a limitation; any later rule change is a dated amendment re-checked on a different subsample.
- Second validation round (amendment 1.8, 24-11-2026). The first round (60 records, seed 20261116; coded 23-24 November 2026) met the threshold for inclusion (kappa 0.68) but not for the accommodation codes (A1 kappa 0.20, AC1 0.19; A2 0.57; A3 0.56). Two sources of disagreement were found: (a) the coder assigned A1 together with A2 to schemes with fault estimation, which the definitions in Section 4.2 exclude; (b) the rules assigned A1 by default when no A term fired (flag A1-FALLBACK). Changes: (a) A1 and A2 are mutually exclusive for one scheme, in the codebook, the coding manual and the rules; (b) when no A code fires, the rules assign A1 only if the text names a fixed robust or adaptive law (sliding mode, terminal sliding mode, super-twisting, adaptive, robust, backstepping, H-infinity, prescribed performance or passive fault tolerance; flag A1-LAW); otherwise A is left unassigned (flag A-NR). The term list was written from the A1 definition and was not tuned on the first-round records. Inclusion decisions and the F, M and E codes do not change (the 2280 decisions are identical; only A and the flags differ, verified by script; v1.7 outputs are kept). The L3 samples, drawn with v1.7 codes, are not redrawn, because L3 is coded from full text.
- Round 2 sample and coding: the author codes blind a new random sample of 60 primary records with rule decision INCLUDE, published 2016-2026, with abstract, excluding the development set, the first-round sample, every L3 record (L3-R, L3-P and the replacements actually extracted) and records by the author (OWN). Stratified by the first rule A code under v1.8: A1 15, A2 15, A3 10, A4 10, A-NR 10 (seed 20261124; if a stratum is short, its deficit goes to A1, then A2). The sheet has the first-round columns except database index keywords, which are not shown; it is delivered as an Excel workbook with drop-down lists. The coding manual states the A1/A2 exclusion with a decision sequence and lets the coder leave A empty when the abstract does not identify the accommodation (the human counterpart of A-NR). Because the coder knows that the frame is rule-INCLUDE, the inclusion decision in round 2 is not blind to the rule and is a secondary outcome only. Coding without tools, artificial intelligence or the key, with a written declaration. The coder is no longer naive about the rule behavior found in round 1; this is declared as a limitation.
- Round 2 analysis, fixed before coding (script rs3h2_acuerdo.py): primary, per-code Cohen's kappa and Gwet's AC1 for A1-A4, with bootstrap 95 % intervals stratified by the rule A stratum (2000 replicates, seed 20261125), on the records the coder includes; estimates are unweighted sample estimates and the stratum sizes are reported. Secondary: F, M and E codes; the share of rule-INCLUDE records the coder includes (Wilson interval); and, post hoc and labeled as such, the first-round agreement recomputed with the v1.8 rules. Both rounds are reported. Decision rule: if the kappa of A1 and of A2 are both at least 0.60, the mapping-set F x A cross-tabulation uses the v1.8 codes as primary, with this agreement reported; otherwise the mapping-set A codes are reported descriptively only and every F x A statement rests on the full-text set (L3). No further rule change is made after round 2.
- Full-text eligibility applies to the synthesis set; exclusions at that stage are listed with reason (Supplementary Table S4).

## 8. Corpus layers
- L1, mapping set: all eligible records of all years, coded by rules (F, A, M, E bin, tags).
- L2, canon: at most 15 foundational works chosen by expert judgment from the pre-2010 citation ranking of L1 and the seed reviews (Visinsky et al. 1994 is the first entry). Each is verified by DOI, publisher or library catalog before it is cited.
- L3, synthesis set (2016-2026), at most 120 studies read at full text from the publisher's HTML:
  - L3-R, probability sample: 60 primary studies drawn at random with a recorded seed, stratified by E bin: 20 per bin; when a bin holds fewer than 20, all its records are taken and the deficit is split evenly between the other bins (v1.5). Estimates use the stratum weights N_h / n_h. It supports unbiased estimates and measures the error of the abstract-based E coding.
  - L3-P, purposive sample: the record with most citations per year (citations divided by 2027 minus publication year) in every non-empty F x A cell, all eligible papers of the target journal, and then the next records by citations per year until 60.
  Per-study data go to Supplementary Table S5 with the sample label. Estimates use L1 and L3-R; L3-P feeds the fault-model library, the comparison tables and the narrative.

## 9. Data items (L3)
Bibliographic data; manipulator (class, degrees of freedom, model); fault class and sub-code, equation as written, magnitude, onset time, profile, number of simultaneous faults; accommodation family and mechanism; sensing used by the accommodation (encoders, joint torque, force/torque, vision, inertial) and fault information required (detection delay, estimator); structural condition (remaining actuation, redundancy degree); stability claim and type; evidence level and platform; metrics with baseline and condition; repetitions and statistics; code and data availability.
FAME reporting checklist, scored 0 or 1 per item: R1 fault equation; R2 magnitude; R3 onset and profile; R4 injection mechanism (software command scaling, hardware disconnection, brake, real damage); R5 nominal and faulty baselines; R6 quantitative metrics; R7 actuator limits; R8 noise or disturbances; R9 repetitions or statistics; R10 code or data available. RS4 may refine the wording; changes are amendments.

## 10. Study appraisal
No clinical risk-of-bias tool applies. Credibility is expressed by the evidence level and the checklist score, reported per study and used as stratifiers.

## 11. Synthesis and statistics
- Descriptive: records per year and source; distributions of F, A, M, E and tags; F x A map with counts, evidence bins and empty cells; F x A by M and by period.
- Structural-condition and sensing table (RQ2): for each fault model, the conditions and the measurements under which each family can restore the task (for example, F4q requires kinematic redundancy or task relaxation; F5 requires underactuated control or braking).
- Confirmatory tests H1-H3: odds ratios with 95 % confidence intervals on L1 (E bins), Holm adjustment across the three tests; repeated on L3-R with full-text E as a check.
- Exploratory associations: odds ratios with intervals, Haldane-Anscombe correction for zero cells, labeled exploratory, no significance claims.
- Measurement error: the L3-R confusion matrix between abstract-based and full-text E gives sensitivity and specificity of the E rule; L1 proportions are reported raw and corrected (Rogan-Gladen) with intervals.
- Sensitivity analyses: WoS only, Scopus only, union; without conference papers; without UNCERTAIN and NO-ABS [superseded by amendment 1.9(a) for UNCERTAIN: the residue is outside the primary mapping set and is added, not removed]; without OWN; 2016-2026 only.
- Exhaustiveness: recall equals in-scope snowball records already in F01 OR SF01 divided by all in-scope snowball records, with Wilson 95 % interval. Target 0.90; below 0.85 triggers a string amendment and a re-run. Scope of the snowball records is decided by the scripted rules; every loss is then read (title and abstract) and classified as out of scope, coverage (source not indexed), indexing delay, or string. Both values are reported: by rules alone and after the manual reading of losses. The trigger applies to the second (v1.5).
- Each results section compares, contrasts and closes with what the literature does not resolve. The Discussion contrasts the findings with the seed reviews and states threats to validity.
- All counts, tables and figures come from saved scripts; a verification script checks every number in the manuscript against the result files.
